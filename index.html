  2. Check the preview and scan it with your own phone camera. It should open exactly what you expect.
  3. Pick a size — 512 px is fine for WhatsApp and screens; choose 1024 or 2048 px for anything printed.
  4. Download the PNG and print it or add it to your poster, menu, visiting card or product label.
Printing rule of thumb: print a payment or shop QR at least 4–5 cm wide, a poster QR much bigger — people scan from a distance. Never shrink a dense QR (long URL + highest error correction) into a tiny space.

Common questions

Does this QR code expire?

No. A static QR holds the content inside the black-and-white pattern itself — there is no server and no expiry. It works as long as the thing it points to (your link, UPI ID, Wi-Fi password) stays the same.

Can I change the link after printing?

No — that's the trade-off of a free static QR. If the destination may change later (a menu, a price list), point the QR at a page you control and update that page instead.

Is my data sent anywhere?

No. The QR is generated entirely in your browser; your text, UPI ID and Wi-Fi password never leave your device. Nothing you type here is stored.

Why won't my QR scan?

Usually one of three things: too much text (use a shorter link), too little contrast (go back to black on white), or printed too small for how dense it is. Lower the error correction to Low and try again.

Can I put my logo in the middle?

Not in this tool. If you add a small logo yourself in an image editor, use High or Highest error correction and test-scan afterwards — the covered centre is exactly what error correction repairs.
